## The Project Story

This 1,200 sqft condominium in the heart of Mont Kiara was reimagined for the discerning expatriate rental market. The previous interior was functional but uninspiring — beige walls, dated cabinetry, and a layout that failed to capitalise on the unit's generous proportions.

### The Challenge

Mont Kiara is one of Malaysia's most competitive expat rental markets. Tenants in this segment have high expectations — they compare units against international standards. The existing fit-out was not meeting the benchmark, resulting in rental rates 18% below comparable units in the same building.

### Our Approach

We identified the target demographic as **Korean and Japanese expat families** — tenants who value minimalist aesthetics, functional storage, and quality materials. The design language was **Scandinavian with warm accents** — light oak tones, clean white surfaces, and strategic pops of muted colour.

Key interventions included:

- **Kitchen reconfiguration**: Opened up the kitchen to the living area with a peninsula island, creating a social hub suited to contemporary family life. Fitted with quartz countertops, soft-close Blum hardware, and integrated appliances
- **Custom wardrobes and storage**: Floor-to-ceiling built-in wardrobes in all bedrooms with internal configurations optimised for family use — deep drawers, double hanging rods, and luggage storage
- **False ceiling and ambient lighting**: Seamless plaster ceiling throughout the living and dining areas with recessed LED downlights and concealed cove lighting, eliminating the harsh single-point ceiling lights
- **Premium painting protocol**: Full Nippon Paint specification with dedicated primer coat, two finishing coats of EasyWash series, and comprehensive wall rectification — patching hairline cracks, skim-coating, and sanding before any paint touched the walls

### The Result

The unit achieved a **+14% rental yield uplift** and was tenanted within three days of listing. The landlord reports significantly lower tenant turnover and maintenance requests compared to their other, non-renovated units.
